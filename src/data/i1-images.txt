# Picture questions, from HY Step 1 Images. Microbiology.
#
#   I|topic|tags|picture file|answer|distractor|explanation
#
# The picture IS the question — no stem, because any wording narrows it down
# before the options do. The answer is whatever the picture actually shows:
# usually the disease, but a picture of a non-specific finding filed under a
# disease (erythema nodosum under histoplasmosis) is answered by the finding,
# with the parent disease named in the explanation.
#
# Left out on purpose: pictures that could be several things (a bare
# granuloma, a chest film of "pneumonia with hilar adenopathy" that is equally
# TB or sarcoid), near-duplicates of a picture already used, and any picture
# with its own answer printed on it — the Aspergillus film on p7 is labelled
# "Aspergilloma" in the image itself.
#
# The source table's column decides the tag: Histology -> histo, CT/MRI/XR ->
# radio, gross photographs take their section tag and a body-system tag. The
# column is only a default: p7's silver stain sits under "Gross" but is
# tagged histo, because that is what it is.
#
# Where one disease has several pictures the topic names the finding, so the
# two do not collide as the same fact.

I|Paracoccidioidomycosis|micro,histo|p03-c.webp|Paracoccidioidomycosis|North American blastomycosis|Many buds ringing one mother cell, the captain's-wheel form. Blastomyces makes a single broad-based bud, never a crown of them.

I|Taenia solium|micro,histo|p05-a.webp|Taenia solium|Diphyllobothrium latum|A scolex crowned with hooklets and suckers. The fish tapeworm attaches by two sucking grooves and has no hooks, and causes B12 deficiency.

I|Candida - germ tubes|micro,histo|p06-a.webp|Candida albicans|Aspergillus fumigatus|Germ tubes sprouting from yeast at 37C, the test that identifies C. albicans. Aspergillus never grows as yeast: it is septate hyphae throughout.
I|Oral candidiasis|micro,gi|p06-c.webp|Oral candidiasis|Oral hairy leukoplakia|Curd-like white plaques on the tongue that scrape off leaving raw mucosa. Hairy leukoplakia is an EBV-driven white plaque on the lateral tongue that cannot be scraped away.
I|Candida - pseudohyphae|micro,histo|p06-d.webp|Candida albicans|Aspergillus fumigatus|Budding yeast strung into pseudohyphae, pinched at each junction. Aspergillus has true septate hyphae of even width and no yeast forms.

I|Aspergillus - conidiophore|micro,histo|p07-c.webp|Aspergillosis|Rhizopus infection|A conidiophore with a swollen vesicle radiating chains of spores. Rhizopus makes sporangia, closed sacs of spores, not radiating chains.
I|Pneumocystis - silver stain|micro,histo,resp|p07-d.webp|Pneumocystis jirovecii|Histoplasmosis|Cup-shaped cysts likened to crushed ping-pong balls. Histoplasma yeasts are smaller, oval, and sit inside macrophages.
I|Pneumocystis - cyst wall|micro,histo,resp|p07-e.webp|Pneumocystis jirovecii|Cryptococcosis|Silver-stained cysts, several showing the collapsed cup shape. Cryptococcus is a budding yeast with a capsule and stains with mucicarmine.
I|Kaposi sarcoma - histology|micro,histo|p08-a.webp|Kaposi sarcoma|Bacillary angiomatosis|Spindle cells forming slit-like vascular spaces with extravasated red cells. Bacillary angiomatosis is the Bartonella mimic and shows plump epithelioid endothelium with neutrophils, and it responds to antibiotics.
I|Kaposi sarcoma - skin|micro,derm|p08-b.webp|Kaposi sarcoma|Angiosarcoma|Violaceous plaques and nodules spreading over the face in advanced HIV, driven by HHV-8. Angiosarcoma of the head and neck occurs in the elderly and is not HHV-8 related.
I|Kaposi sarcoma - oral|micro,gi|p08-c.webp|Kaposi sarcoma|Pyogenic granuloma|A flat violaceous lesion on the hard palate, one of the commonest sites for oral Kaposi sarcoma. A pyogenic granuloma is a solitary raised bleeding nodule, usually on the gingiva after trauma.
I|HIV encephalitis|micro,histo,neuro|p09-a.webp|HIV encephalitis|Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy|Multinucleated giant cells in brain parenchyma, formed by fusion of infected microglia and macrophages. PML is a JC virus demyelination with bizarre astrocytes and enlarged oligodendrocyte nuclei, without giant cells.
I|Croup|micro,radio,resp|p09-c.webp|Croup|Epiglottitis|Subglottic narrowing on a frontal neck film, the steeple sign of parainfluenza croup. Epiglottitis is seen on the lateral film as a swollen epiglottis, the thumbprint sign.
I|Measles - Koplik spots|micro,gi|p10-a.webp|Measles|Herpangina|Small white specks on an erythematous buccal mucosa, appearing a day or two before the measles rash. Herpangina gives grey vesicles and ulcers on the soft palate and tonsillar pillars, caused by coxsackievirus A.
I|Leishmaniasis - amastigotes|micro,histo|p10-b.webp|Leishmaniasis|Histoplasmosis|Macrophages filled with amastigotes, each with a nucleus and a rod-shaped kinetoplast. Histoplasma is also intracellular but has no kinetoplast and stains with silver.
I|Leishmaniasis - promastigotes|micro,histo|p10-c.webp|Leishmaniasis|African trypanosomiasis|Slender flagellated promastigotes, the form grown in culture and carried by the sandfly. Trypanosoma brucei circulates in blood as a trypomastigote with an undulating membrane along its whole length.
I|Actinomyces|micro,histo|p11-b.webp|Actinomyces israelii|Nocardia asteroides|Gram-positive branching filaments. Nocardia looks similar but is partially acid-fast, aerobic, and causes disease in the immunosuppressed rather than after dental work.
I|Actinomyces - sinus tract|micro,derm|p11-c.webp|Actinomycosis|Scrofula|A draining sinus in the jaw and neck discharging yellow sulfur granules, typically after dental trauma. Scrofula is mycobacterial cervical lymphadenitis: matted painless nodes that may ulcerate but discharge no granules.
I|Trypanosoma cruzi|micro,histo|p11-d.webp|Chagas disease|Malaria|C-shaped trypomastigotes lying free between red cells in the acute phase. Plasmodium is inside the red cells, as rings or as gametocytes, never swimming between them.

I|Cardiac myxoma - gross|cardio|p13-b.webp|Cardiac myxoma|Mural thrombus|A pale gelatinous pedunculated mass in the left atrium, the classic site. It can obstruct the mitral valve and give a tumour plop. A mural thrombus is laminated red-brown and sits flat against the wall.
